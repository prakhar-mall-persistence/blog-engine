What Retell AI is

Retell AI is a platform for building voice agents. A voice agent is software that answers or places phone calls and holds a spoken conversation. You configure what it should say and do, connect it to a phone number, and it handles calls without a human on the line.

How Retell AI works

A voice agent chains three parts together. First, speech-to-text converts what the caller says into words. Second, a language model reads those words, plus the conversation so far and your instructions, and decides the reply. Third, text-to-speech turns that reply back into audio the caller hears.

This loop runs on every turn of the conversation. The caller speaks, the system transcribes, the model responds, and the voice plays back. Between those steps the platform also decides when the caller has stopped talking, so it knows when to reply. That decision is called turn-taking or endpointing.

The words the model picks are rarely the hard part. Modern language models write fine replies. The hard part is doing all three steps fast enough that the caller does not notice a delay, and handling interruptions when someone talks over the agent.

How to retell a story in your own words

Since the keyword overlaps, a quick honest answer for the other audience: to retell a story in your own words, name the characters, say where it happens, then walk through the beginning, middle, and end in order. Skip small details and keep the main events.

For kids and for grade 3, the same structure works: who, where, what happened first, next, and last. Have them use the story's key words but their own sentences. Sites like understood.org and speechymusings.com cover this in depth.

What to look for when you evaluate a voice platform

Latency is the first thing to test. Measure how long the agent takes to start replying after the caller stops talking. Anything over a second feels slow on a phone call. Test it on real calls, not demos, because network conditions change the number.

Turn-taking is the second. A good agent lets the caller interrupt and stops talking when they do. A weak one keeps speaking over the caller or waits too long before replying. Both feel broken.

The third is whether you can build, test, and monitor in one place. If building the agent, testing it against real scenarios, and watching live calls all live in separate tools, you lose time stitching them together. A pitfall with any platform is pricing that hides behind a sales call, so check whether you can see rates and start a pilot without a contract.
